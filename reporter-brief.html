Reporter-ready brief

Cursor denies an advertised paid Grok Bot benefit after X authorization succeeds.

The subscriber accepts a new matching personal account. That path is still blocked, and support loops between Cursor and xAI.

Unresolved

The story in one sentence

A paid SuperGrok Heavy identity approves Cursor's X/xAI authorization request, but Cursor responds with policy_denied, cannot complete the required matching-.ru personal-account route, and has supplied no precise backend reason or working remedy.

What works

The paid X/xAI identity reaches Cursor's consent screen and approves identity and email access.

What fails

Cursor's callback rejects the session and Grok Bot displays “Access blocked, please contact support.”

Confirmed facts

  • xAI publicly lists SuperGrok Heavy among plans that include Grok Bot.
  • Cursor's public linking guide confirms individual Heavy eligibility, a one-account usage grant without a paid Cursor-plan prerequisite, permanent linking, and support routing.
  • The paid identity uses a valid .ru email address and authenticates through X.
  • The consent step was reached and approved.
  • Cursor returned policy_denied.
  • The failure survived fresh sessions, ordinary proxy/VPN checks, and a reported settings refresh.
  • Cursor support said emails must match and successful links cannot be moved.
  • A new matching personal Cursor account is acceptable to the subscriber.
  • xAI assigned Cursor support responsibility; Cursor automation closed the transferred request as duplicate.

Not yet confirmed

  • Whether .ru is the actual decision trigger.
  • Whether a permanent, reserved, or partial link exists.
  • Whether Cursor, xAI, or a shared service made the denial.
  • Whether the denial reflects policy, risk scoring, regional controls, or an account defect.
  • What unpublished rule, if any, supplements the public guides, which identify no same-email or .ru-domain restriction.

Why this is newsworthy

  • A premium subscription advertises a partner benefit that cannot be activated.
  • The identity flow succeeds far enough for the partner to evaluate and deny it.
  • The same-email plus permanent-link requirements make unsupported workarounds dangerous.
  • Cross-company routing has not produced one accountable technical owner.
  • The apparent domain-sensitive behavior is not explained in the public plan material.

Questions for Cursor and xAI

  1. What exact backend rule produced policy_denied?
  2. Can a personal Cursor account use the matching .ru address?
  3. If not, where is that restriction published and what remedy applies?
  4. Does any existing or partial permanent link exist?
  5. Which company owns entitlement delivery, eligibility, and billing remediation?
  6. What is the safe procedure and ETA?
